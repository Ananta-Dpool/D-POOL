## 1. Product Vision

The Campus Marketplace is a **campus-verified, hyperlocal marketplace** where students can buy and sell:

- Used items
- New items
- Handmade products
- Academic/project materials
- Student project work
- Room and hostel items

The initial product is designed around **local, face-to-face transactions**, rather than shipping and logistics.

### Product Positioning

The platform should not initially be positioned as simply "OLX for college students."

Instead:

> **A verified campus exchange network where students can quickly turn unused items into money and find affordable items within their student community.**

The initial strategy is to achieve **high liquidity on one campus and one primary category** before expanding.

---

## 2. Problem

Students regularly buy and sell textbooks, electronics, furniture, project materials, hostel items and other products through fragmented channels such as:

- WhatsApp groups
- Instagram
- General classifieds
- Informal student networks
- Word of mouth

These channels create several problems:

- Listings are difficult to search.
- Important posts get buried quickly.
- There is no consistent student verification.
- Buyers and sellers may not know each other.
- There is no structured marketplace reputation.
- Transactions are disconnected from campus life.
- Students often have difficulty discovering what is available nearby.

### Core Problem Statement

> Students need a trusted, searchable and campus-focused way to discover, buy and sell items within their student community without the complexity of traditional marketplaces.

## 3. Target Users

### 3.1 Primary Users

- College and university students
- Approximately 18–25 years old
- Students living on or around campus
- Students who frequently buy or sell low-to-medium-value items

### 3.2 Primary Seller Types

- Students selling used textbooks
- Students selling project materials
- Students moving hostels/rooms
- Graduating students clearing belongings
- Students reselling electronics and accessories
- Students selling handmade or student-created products

### 3.3 Primary Buyer Types

- Students looking for affordable second-hand products
- First-year students setting up hostel rooms
- Students looking for academic materials
- Students looking for project supplies
- Students looking for products within their campus community

### 3.4 Future Users

- Alumni
- Campus organizations
- Local businesses serving students

These groups should not be a priority for the first launch.

## 4. Product Solution

The product combines five core ideas:

1. **Student verification**
2. **Campus-first discovery**
3. **Simple listing and search**
4. **Direct buyer-seller communication**
5. **Face-to-face transactions**

### Key Differentiators

- Campus-verified users
- One account for buying and selling
- Default campus feed
- Cross-campus browsing later
- No mandatory shipping
- Flexible payment
- Structured transaction records
- Campus-safe meetup locations
- Student reputation

---

## 5. Core Principles

1. **One account, dual capability** — every user can buy and sell.
2. **Campus-first, campus-verified** — trust comes from verified student status.
3. **Meet in person** — no shipping or logistics in the initial product.
4. **Payment flexibility** — cash/UPI at meetup initially; platform payments later.
5. **Liquidity before scale** — make one campus work before expanding.
6. **Category before catalog** — dominate one high-demand category first.
7. **Simple first** — avoid building complex features before validating demand.
8. **Trust by design** — verification, reporting and transaction history are built into the product.

### 9.3 Personalized Notifications

Notifications should be introduced as an **opt-in, interest-based system** that connects relevant supply with user demand.

The system can learn interests from user activity such as:

- Searches
- Listing views
- Saved listings
- Chats
- Purchases
- Manually selected interests

A simple scoring system should be sufficient initially. Advanced AI recommendations can be introduced later.

#### Interest Profile

Conceptually:

```text
User activity
   ↓
Searches / views / saves / chats / purchases
   ↓
Interest profile
   ↓
Relevant new listings
   ↓
Notification preferences
   ↓
Email notification
```

#### Notification Types

**New Listing Alerts**

Notify users when a newly listed item strongly matches their interests.

Example:

> A new calculator matching your interests was listed on your campus.

**Wanted-Item Matches**

When a user searches for an unavailable item, remember that demand and notify them when a relevant listing appears.

**Price Alerts**

Notify users when a relevant item becomes cheaper or falls within a saved price range.

**Category Digests**

Instead of sending an email for every listing, group relevant items into a daily or weekly digest.

Example:

> **5 new items you may like this week**

**Seller Activity Notifications**

Provide useful information such as:

- Listing views
- New buyer interest
- New messages
- Listing performance

Transaction-related emails remain separate from recommendation emails.

#### Notification Preferences

Users should be able to choose:

- Instant alerts
- Daily digest
- Weekly digest
- No promotional/recommendation emails

Essential transactional notifications should remain separate from marketing/recommendation preferences where appropriate.

#### Frequency Control

The system must prevent notification overload.

Recommended controls:

- Per-user notification frequency limits
- Relevance threshold before sending
- Deduplication of similar alerts
- Digest grouping
- Quiet periods / suppression windows in later versions

The goal is **useful discovery, not inbox spam**.

A campus event where students are helped to list several items immediately.

Target:

> **5–10 seed sellers → 50+ listings**

The goal is to prevent a "dead marketplace" experience for new users.

## 10. Trust & Safety

Trust is a core product feature, not an optional add-on.

### 10.1 Student Verification

Initial:

- College email domain verification
- Manual admin review where required

Later:

- ID card upload
- OCR-assisted verification

### 10.2 User Roles

```text
Student
Campus Admin
Platform Admin
```

Campus administrators should not automatically receive platform-wide administrative access.

### 10.3 Safe Meetups

Recommended meetup locations should prioritize:

- Public campus areas
- Well-lit locations
- CCTV-covered areas where available
- Areas with regular student activity

A full interactive Safe Zone map can be introduced after the pilot.

### 10.4 Deal Check-in

Future flow:

```text
Buyer: "I've arrived"
        ↓
Seller: "I've arrived"
        ↓
Deal completed
        ↓
Transaction closed
```

### 10.5 Banned Items

Examples:

- Alcohol
- Drugs
- Weapons
- Exam papers
- Fake IDs
- Other prohibited or illegal goods

### 10.6 Reporting

Users should be able to report:

- Listing
- User
- Message
- Suspicious transaction

## 11. Business Model

### Phase 1–2

The priority is **liquidity, not revenue**.

Avoid introducing unnecessary payment friction during the first pilot.

Students can initially settle transactions directly through:

- Cash
- UPI

### Phase 3+

#### 1. Listing Boosts

Low-cost featured placement for sellers.

#### 2. Transaction Commission

Potentially 2–5% on platform-mediated payments, subject to payment costs, legal requirements and actual user willingness to pay.

#### 3. Campus Advertising

Potential advertisers:

- Print shops
- Cafes
- Stationery stores
- Hostels
- Student service providers

## 14. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fake students | College email verification + later ID verification |
| No listings | Seed sellers + Listing Parties |
| Marketplace feels empty | Launch with 50–100 listings |
| Low demand | Focus on one high-demand entry category |
| No-show meetups | Transaction tracking, reporting and future check-ins |
| Low trust | Verification, reputation and safe meetup locations |
| Users stay on WhatsApp | Better search, discovery and structured listings |
| Too many features | Strict MVP scope |
| Payment complexity | Start with direct cash/UPI |
| Legal liability | Clear Terms, banned-item policy and dispute process |
| Poor liquidity | Track time-to-sell and successful transaction rate |
| Notification fatigue | Opt-in controls, relevance thresholds, frequency limits and digests |
| Irrelevant recommendations | Activity-based scoring, feedback signals and suppression rules |
| Admin abuse | Separate Student, Campus Admin and Platform Admin roles |

## 15. Launch Strategy

### Principle

> **One campus. One category. High liquidity. Then expand.**

### Stage 1 — Seed Supply

Recruit:

- 5–10 seed sellers
- 50+ initial listings

Prioritize the selected entry category.

### Stage 2 — Controlled Launch

Launch to approximately:

- 100–200 verified students

Observe:

- What they search for
- What they list
- How quickly items receive chats
- How many conversations become transactions

### Stage 3 — Improve Liquidity

Focus on:

- Better categories
- Better search
- Better listing quality
- More supply
- Wanted posts
- Campus promotion

### Stage 4 — Expand

Only consider the next campus when the first campus demonstrates repeatable transaction activity.

## Product Success Definition

The first version is successful **not when it has thousands of users**, but when students on one campus repeatedly use it to complete real transactions.
